# Attenstra Web Presence And Audience Capture Summary V1

## Purpose

Capture the current operating guidance for how Attenstra.ai should present
itself on the web and collect audience interest as an early-stage AI venture.

## Summary

As of April 4, 2026, best-in-class for an early-stage AI venture is not a big
"company website." It is a sharp trust-and-capture system:

1. one clear story
2. one primary CTA
3. one durable owned audience channel: email

For Attenstra.ai, the strongest pattern is:

- A focused landing page, not a multi-page brochure.
- Clear category language in the first screen: what Attenstra is, who it is
  for, and why it matters now.
- A primary CTA of `Join the waitlist` or `Get product updates`, not `Book a
  demo` unless you already want sales calls.
- A lightweight email capture form above the fold.
- A short privacy explanation at point of capture.
- Ongoing proof of life after signup: product notes, founder updates, milestone
  emails, and occasional substantive thinking.

What best-in-class sites do well:

- They avoid vague "AI transformation" language and explain the problem in plain
  English.
- They sell a thesis before they sell features.
- They use proof signals early: founder credibility, product screenshots,
  architectural point of view, pilot interest, or clear roadmap posture.
- They keep friction low. Mailchimp still recommends asking only for essential
  info at signup and being explicit about what subscribers will receive and how
  often. It also recommends double opt-in when list quality and deliverability
  matter.
- They treat privacy correctly. ICO guidance is explicit that "I read the
  privacy policy" is not the same as consent for marketing; the marketing choice
  must be separate and clear.

For audience capture specifically, best practice is:

- Primary conversion: email waitlist.
- Secondary conversion: founder or content follow on LinkedIn or X.
- Tertiary conversion: optional "request a conversation" for investors, design
  partners, or technical collaborators.
- Capture only `email` first. Add optional fields only if they help
  segmentation.
- Segment immediately on intent if possible: investor, operator, engineer,
  design partner, general follower.
- Send a confirmation or welcome email immediately.
- Tell people exactly what they will get: "occasional product and company
  updates," not generic "news."
- Build from a permission-based list only. No purchased or scraped audiences.

A strong early-stage AI startup site in 2026 usually has these sections:

- Hero: what the company is and why it matters.
- Thesis or problem: what is broken in the world.
- Product direction: what PACE and the portfolio are aiming to become.
- Credibility: founder and company signals, technical posture, or proof points.
- Waitlist block: short form, privacy note, clear expectations.
- Optional FAQ: useful for humans and search or AI retrieval.

What not to do:

- Do not launch with six nav items and thin pages.
- Do not make the homepage investor-only unless fundraising is the immediate
  priority.
- Do not hide the email CTA below a long scroll.
- Do not collect name, company, role, use case, and phone unless there is a
  concrete reason.
- Do not sound like a pitch deck. Startup sites convert better when the
  language is concrete.

## Attenstra-Specific Recommendation

For Attenstra.ai, the recommended posture is:

- Position Attenstra as the company behind PACE.
- Make the homepage about governed AI systems people can trust.
- Offer a waitlist for product and company updates.
- Add a small "for investors or design partners" path, but keep it secondary.
- Publish regular public build signals so the list has a reason to grow and
  stay warm.
